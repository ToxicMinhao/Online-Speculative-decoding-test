- [ ] **Answer both comparisons separately on each panel.** Lead with KRC4 versus O4, then K4 versus O4 and KRC4 versus K4. Show counts, `100 × (baseline − method) / baseline`, request wins/losses/ties and gross calls saved/added. Verify gross saved minus gross added equals the difference in pooled totals. - [ ] **Distinguish relative gaps from KRC's own learning.** Display historical events 1/2 and fresh event 4, and compare the KRC/O and KRC/K gaps in percentage points. Separately show KRC2→KRC4 movement. KRC may improve while its relative gap shrinks or reverses because a control improves more. Additional O1/O2 remain absent, so an additional-panel event-4 win against O supports an endpoint comparison, not demonstrated persistence against O.

- [ ] **Use proportionate wording for tiny or mixed correction effects.** If KRC beats O but its gap against K is small, mixed or reversed, state both facts together with the signed magnitudes. For example, only if the results support it: “KRC4 improves on O4 on both panels, while its incremental call-count advantage over K4 is small and mixed. This trajectory supports the full method against O without establishing a consistent correction advantage.” Here “advantage,” “shrinkage” and “reversal” describe observed call counts, not statistical significance or reliability across training seeds. Do not claim equivalence, no practical effect, universal correction superiority or a preferred future correction schedule. No new magnitude threshold or statistical test is introduced during this review.

The shared O-generated feedback and extra reference computation remain part of the tested recipe. Cross-campaign historical-versus-fresh latency is not a learning curve, and historical O/K training costs are not contemporaneously matched controls.
