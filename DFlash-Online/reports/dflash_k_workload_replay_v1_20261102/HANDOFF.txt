1. QUESTION AND SCOPE

Implement and execute one fixed, eight-event experiment. Does selecting a small set of past verified responses to cover the current workload improve K under the same number of optimizer steps and response presentations? The comparator with uniform replay distinguishes useful selection from the ordinary benefit of replay.

This is a new algorithm implementation experiment, not another test of whether K beats O. Run only K, U and R below. O and K_HALF are unnecessary for this question. The declared stream deliberately switches between math and coding and returns to both; it is a controlled recurrence experiment, not evidence about naturally occurring conversation traffic. The final evaluation panel has been used in development before; do not call it a new unseen confirmatory panel. This fresh eight-event experiment does not measure an improvement over the trained K58 checkpoint. Its percentage gains cannot be added to PR23's full-GSM8K result.

5. COMMON BOUNDED MEMORY

Capacity is fixed at 128 complete prepared response packets. This is an upfront storage-conscious choice. A uniform reservoir retains older workloads in expectation despite a two-event domain change; a FIFO buffer this small would discard all responses from the previous domain.

Use ordinary Algorithm R over strictly past fresh records, one independent PCG64 generator initialized from SeedSequence([20261102,0,2]) for the whole campaign. Fill the first 128 slots. For the nth unique fresh record thereafter (n is one-based), draw j uniformly from 0..n-1 and replace slot j only if j<128. Process insertion in global fresh-record order, not shuffled training order. Save its RNG/counter/slot mapping as scientific state.

During event e, both replay arms see the identical reservoir left after event e-1. Current-event records are not eligible as old replay. Insert the current 128 records only after all three arms have committed and verified that event. Selection cannot change memory admission or retention priority. Do not replay more than one copy of a stored response in the same event. Keep compact descriptors, original identities, selection and membership receipts for all 1,024 records; retain large feature tensors only while current or in this reservoir. Report actual memory domain mixture and age throughout; uniform membership does not guarantee exact domain balance in this seed.

6. COVERAGE SELECTOR — NO NEW MODEL PASSES

For every fresh packet x, read h_x = target_last_hidden_states[0, prompt_length-1, :] from its already-required prepared feedback packet. This is the frozen target's last prompt-token representation, before response tokens. The native capture already supplies this row. It requires no separate target/draft forward or backward and does not inspect future requests, evaluation examples, source answers, domain labels or current draft loss.

On CPU, convert to float64, require finite values and positive norm, and store z_x = h_x / ||h_x||_2. Use all dimensions; no projection or learned encoder. Define s(x,i)=max(0, clip(dot(z_x,z_i),-1,1)). For the current 128 fresh requests Q and a selected old set S, F(S)=(1/128) sum over x in Q of max over i in S of s(x,i), with the empty maximum equal to 0.

Starting from empty S, choose the remaining memory record giving the largest increase in F, repeat exactly 32 times, and resolve exact numeric ties by smaller original global record ordinal. Keep choosing when the remaining gain is zero. This picks past requests that collectively represent the current batch; it is not a loss or acceptance oracle. Use the actual fixed CPU arithmetic in the receipt and record F after every addition. The facility-coverage objective is a proxy. Higher coverage is not itself evidence of model accuracy or decoding speed.

Log the two selections' coverage scores, similarity distribution and concentration, per-step marginal gains, record ages, descriptor norm checks and reporting-only domain composition. No outcome-driven change to the descriptor, clipping, memory capacity, replay count or selector is allowed inside this run.

11. PREDECLARED NEXT ACTION FROM EACH OUTCOME

A. R beats U and K on the final panel, both paired intervals support a positive call reduction, future-event comparisons are directionally compatible, and common-mode timing/costs are acceptable: retain the mechanism unchanged and move to longer validation with another training seed and workload order. The next step is scaling a demonstrated update, not another local diagnostic probe. A later matched full-training experiment is required before claiming an addition to the PR23 K58 gain.

B. U and R improve over K but an R-over-U gain is not established: keep uniform replay as the practical candidate and remove the unsupported coverage-selector claim. A wide R/U interval means uncertainty, not demonstrated equivalence. Use the recorded descriptor and selection evidence to decide whether a distinctly better selector has a concrete rationale; no automatic selector search follows.

C. R improves over U but fails to improve over K: the selector is better than random within an unsuccessful replay allocation. If the future-serving records specifically show recurrence benefits accompanied by loss of fresh-domain adaptation, the next proposed mechanism change is a smaller replay dose, 16 old plus 112 fresh in pass two. Without that evidence, stop this replay allocation. Do not execute the new dose in this campaign.

D. R helps current-domain/future requests but hurts the other domain enough to lose the balanced final test: identify a specialization-versus-retention tradeoff. A concrete next candidate, if the per-domain evidence supports it, is to divide the same 32 replay slots into 16 coverage-selected and 16 uniformly selected from the remaining memory. This keeps the budget fixed while adding an explicit retention component. Do not execute this candidate here or call the balanced endpoint positive.

E. Both replay arms lose to K with no supported recurrence benefit, or R increases coverage without improving useful serving: retain K and stop this mechanism in its current form. If the descriptors do not discriminate the workload, report that the fixed descriptor was uninformative. If coverage rises without serving gain, report that this coverage proxy did not improve the draft. Do not change the descriptor post hoc and present a selected rerun as the original result.

F. Calls improve but common-mode serving time does not: report call efficiency and inspect the measured serving costs before claiming speedup. If selector/I/O/updater overhead is material, optimize the measured overhead while freezing the scientific selection rule; updater cost is separate from the measured C=1 serving latency. The experiment does not measure how asynchronous update lag affects deployment benefit.

G. Valid effects remain inconclusive: finish and report uncertainty. If both domains and the future-serving records show a practically useful, consistent direction, the next possible step is one unchanged replication with another training seed. Otherwise park the candidate. Neither case authorizes a chain of small probes, automatic budget extensions or tuning on the final panel.

If several descriptions apply, report all of them and recommend one next action supported by the complete evidence. Invalid runs are implementation failures, not negative efficacy results.
