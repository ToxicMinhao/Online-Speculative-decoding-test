## Statistical interpretation coverage: 11/11 reviewed

This scan checks the stated interpretation, not whether every possible bias has been eliminated.

| Risk | Finding and limit |
|---|---|
| Simpson's paradox | Raw and adjusted coefficients have the same positive sign. Response and joint-stratum effects are controlled; no claim covers every unexamined subgroup. |
| Ecological fallacy | Blocks are clustered by response. The report does not treat 8,192 occurrences as independent experiments or extrapolate to model populations. |
| Berkson/selection bias | HIGH eligibility is fixed from the parent, not child success. Conclusions remain conditional on this selected cohort; selection-induced associations are not ruled out. |
| Collider bias | Controls precede the child outcome, and child repair/survival is not conditioned on. The observational design does not establish causal identification or exclude all collider structures. |
| Base-rate neglect | Counts and denominators are reported. The score is not described as a calibrated probability. |
| Regression to the mean | Parent-error selection and one before/after transition cannot distinguish every natural regression or shared-learning effect. No causal allocation benefit is claimed. |
| Survivorship bias | All 128 responses and selected blocks remain in coverage; regressions remain in the primary outcome. No invalid or incomplete native work was hidden. |
| Look-elsewhere effect | One prespecified full-model interval determines the conclusion. Reduced, raw and binary results are descriptive; none replaces the inconclusive primary result. |
| Forking paths | Cohort, anchors, score, controls, rank/support rules and bootstrap were sealed before new outcomes. No category merging, score tuning or replacement of invalid draws occurred. |
| Correlation versus causation | The report states adjusted association only. It makes no claim of improved learning, an optimal weighting law or serving gain. |
| Reverse causality | Predictors are computed from the parent and outcomes from the child. Temporal ordering does not remove confounding; no causal direction is inferred. |
