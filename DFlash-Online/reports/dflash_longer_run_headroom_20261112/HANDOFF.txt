Executor handoff | Does K+ leave useful repairs inside its high-priority group?

Run one frozen-checkpoint diagnostic on GSM8K and MathQA. K+ already gives extra weight to a first error whose next proposal is correct. Determine whether errors with two or more consecutive correct followers offer enough remaining, realizable benefit to justify a finer allocation. Deliver a decision and its supporting counts. Scope ends at diagnosis: zero optimizer steps, zero serving benchmarks, no candidate training.

3. Measure remaining opportunity and realized payoff

At the same start compare parent and child to the canonical tokens. “Intact” means every slot before j still matches. “Repair” means intact plus the child matches j and native verification advances past the old accepted prefix. For each dataset and group report:

• Still blocked: intact, j still wrong. Among LONG, count how often the child also retains the first two correct followers. This is an opportunity a further repair could unlock.
• Realized: among repaired LONG blockers, count how often both original followers actually survive and are accepted. Give the numerator and repair denominator. A missed repair contributes zero to the unconditional realized count.
• Collateral and donor cost: report earlier prefix breaks and signed native accepted-prefix change for every start, including gains, losses and ties. Show repairs, breaks and net tokens for SHORT too; it would give up training weight in the proposed refinement.

Report two planning quantities: (A) LONG’s exact share of all later parent rounds × the sampled LONG fraction still blocked with both followers intact; (B) the fraction of repaired LONG cases that actually accept both followers. Show distinct contributing requests and 95% request-cluster bootstrap intervals by dataset (10,000 draws; seed 20261112). Recompute census shares and sampled ratios together. Report undefined bootstrap draws; empty or sparse repair denominators and degenerate zero-event intervals are insufficient to rule out a payoff. A census-confirmed lack of eligible LONG starts can rule out that opportunity. For SHORT/LONG comparisons also show results standardized to observed common (j,v) cells, weighted by the smaller parent census count in each cell. Show common-support coverage; sparse cells cannot establish a difference. Do not translate local token gains into saved verifier calls.

4. Report the decision

Use 1 in 100 later starts for A and half of repaired LONG cases for B as proposed engineering thresholds. They screen whether it is worth spending a training run; neither is a predicted speedup. If both workloads clear both thresholds (lower interval bounds above them) and the events recur across requests, mark the serving evidence as supporting a candidate test. A well-resolved shortfall closes this cross-workload rationale. Intervals straddling a threshold, a missing group, conflicting workloads or incomplete replay leave the corresponding conclusion unresolved. Report dataset-specific findings even if the joint screen fails.

Separately check retained training-anchor records if available. Compute the SHORT/LONG shares of native high-priority weight and how much lies in response/depth groups containing both, relative to all selected weight. Serving-round frequencies use a different anchor distribution. If per-anchor labels or weights are unavailable, report training-weight headroom as unknown; a later candidate would need to qualify that the reallocation actually moves weight. This probe cannot show that moving weight helps.

Return REPORT.md, census and sealed sample, paired per-start rows, rates/intervals, training-anchor coverage, replay/parity receipts and CPU/A800 costs. Independently recompute denominators and signed gains from those rows. A favorable result justifies proposing one capped reallocation within K+’s existing high-priority weight budget, judged against native K+ on complete requests. Stop after the diagnostic.
