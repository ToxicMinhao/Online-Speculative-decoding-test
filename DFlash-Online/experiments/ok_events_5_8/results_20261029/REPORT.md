## Result

**K's verification-call advantage persists at events 4, 6 and 8, but does not grow steadily.** Its net advantage is smaller at event 8 than event 4 on both existing panels. On the prespecified additional fresh panel, K8 uses **3.34% fewer calls** and **3.22% less complete-request time** than O8.

Positive reductions favor K. Calls use one validated trajectory per request, after checking all three repeats. The primary reduction is `100 × (sum O calls − sum K calls) / sum O calls`; panels are kept separate.

## Setup, work and validity

O and K restored their own complete event-4 weights, BF16 Adam state/counters, RNG and history at 512 cumulative steps. Each received four new shared feedback windows, generated by the preceding O checkpoint, with 128 responses per window and unchanged two-pass training. O uses all-valid-proposal forward KL; K uses its own current correct prefix plus the first mismatch, with its qualified weighted response normalization. Both finish at **1,024 cumulative Adam steps and 2,048 cumulative response presentations**. This is shared-feedback objective comparison, not two independently measured online deployments.

The qualified Qwen3-8B/DFlash-b16 models, fixed revisions, BF16 arithmetic, constant LR `3e-4`, AdamW weight decay `0.01`, clipping, Flex attention, maximum 512 anchors/response, accumulation of two separately normalized responses, seed-17 inherited RNG and 7,424-step horizon were preserved. Serving retained greedy block-16 decoding, 2,048 tokens or EOS, context 4,096, concurrency 1, disabled capture and the established cache policy. No training or artifact transfer ran concurrently with timed serving.

| New work | Completed |
|---|---:|
| O-generated feedback responses | 512 |
| Ordinary presentations across O/K | 2,048 |
| AdamW steps across O/K | 1,024 |
| Evaluation generations | 1,920 |
| Total complete-response generations | 2,432 |
| Extra qualification optimizer steps / generation warmups | 0 / 0 |

All **1,920/1,920** evaluation executions were valid: zero invalid, pending, unknown or unattempted. Outputs and termination match each panel's canonical reference; each checkpoint's counts agree across its three repeats. The first eight development requests at event 6 formed the included 48-execution gate. Development uses retained F references; additional uses retained K1 references; fresh uses its first O8 output, with deferred validation when K ran first. The fresh reference therefore checks agreement under the native verifier, not independent target-only decoding.

Presentation order and normal sampled anchors match between arms in every event. K selected 58.74%, 59.77%, 58.97% and 59.84% of available supervised occurrences in events 5–8. These are occurrence counts, not distinct token positions. Training exposure is counted once per completed two-response accumulation window; anchor metadata is counted per presentation. All 512 feedback responses ended at EOS; there were zero output-cap hits. Teacher preparation is separately recorded and does not create additional complete responses.

All requests were sealed before execution without inspecting outcomes. “Fresh” means new to this current study after excluding training, evaluation and probe inventories: 63/64 fresh-panel requests have recorded older unrelated-project exposure; the remaining request has no recorded older exposure, not proof of global novelty. Older-project use was permitted and does not change the sealed panel.
